The Cell · Chapter 7

The Chaperonin Barrel and the Proteasome

GroEL-GroES Hydrophobic Cages, Ubiquitin Death Tags, and the 20S Proteolytic Shredder

Christian Anfinsen showed in 1961 that denatured ribonuclease A refolds spontaneously in a dilute glass beaker, proving that amino acid sequence dictates three-dimensional structure. Inside a living Escherichia coli cell, however, macromolecular crowding reaches 350 milligrams per milliliter. Exposed hydrophobic patches on nascent chains clump into insoluble aggregates within seconds unless shielded by chaperonins.

· · ·

Paul Sigler and Arthur Horwich solved the $2.8\text{-\AA}$ X-ray crystal structure of the bacterial GroEL chaperonin in 1994. Weighing 800 kilodaltons, GroEL is a double-barreled cylinder built from two back-to-back rings of seven subunits each. Its open rims display sticky hydrophobic jaws that catch misfolded polypeptides before they can aggregate.

· · ·

When seven ATP molecules and the heptameric GroES dome cap dock onto a protein-loaded GroEL ring, the apical domains twist 60 degrees upward and 90 degrees clockwise. That rearrangement doubles the cavity volume to $175,000\text{ \AA}^3$ and buries the hydrophobic jaws inside the wall. The trapped $50\text{-kDa}$ protein sits inside a hydrophilic Anfinsen cage to fold alone.

· · ·

The GroEL-GroES folding chamber runs on a strict 10-second clock set by ATP hydrolysis. Once the seven ATP molecules in the capped cis ring hydrolyze to ADP, they allosterically unlock the opposite trans ring to accept a new client and ATP. Binding at the trans ring immediately pops off the cis GroES lid and ejects the protein.

· · ·

Rather than acting merely as a passive isolation box, GroEL actively unfolds kinetically trapped tangles before releasing them into the cavity. Helen Saibil and Franz-Ulrich Hartl demonstrated this mechanical rescue using cryo-EM and FRET. As the seven apical domains twist open during ATP and GroES binding, they pull outward on the substrate to stretch apart wrong $\beta$-sheet misfolds.

· · ·

In the late 1970s, Avram Hershko, Aaron Ciechanover, and Irwin Rose investigated a thermodynamic puzzle: why does destroying unwanted intracellular proteins require ATP when peptide bond hydrolysis is energetically downhill? Fractionating rabbit reticulocyte extracts, they solved the mystery. ATP is spent to covalently weld a 76-amino-acid polypeptide death tag—ubiquitin—onto lysine residues of target proteins.

· · ·

Ubiquitin conjugation proceeds through a three-step enzymatic relay: two human E1 activating enzymes pass ubiquitin to roughly 40 E2 conjugating enzymes, which partner with over 600 distinct E3 ubiquitin ligases. Each E3 ligase recognizes a specific molecular degron on a worn-out or regulatory protein. It then strings together a polyubiquitin chain linked through lysine-48.

· · ·

Once marked with a chain of four or more lysine-48-linked ubiquitins, a condemned protein is delivered to the $2.5\text{-MDa}$ $26\text{S}$ proteasome. Solved at atomic resolution by Robert Huber and Wolfgang Baumeister in 1995, its central $20\text{S}$ core particle stacks four heptameric rings ($\alpha_7\beta_7\beta_7\alpha_7$) into a hollow barrel. That sealed chamber isolates the proteolytic active sites from the cytoplasm.

· · ·

If the $20\text{S}$ proteasome barrel stayed open to the cytosol, it would chew up every healthy protein in sight. To prevent accidental carnage, the N-terminal tails of the two outer $\alpha_7$ rings interlace across the ends of the cylinder. Those tails seal the entry port down to a $1.3\text{-nm}$ gated pore until a $19\text{S}$ regulatory cap unlocks it.

· · ·

Mounted at either end of the $20\text{S}$ barrel, the $19\text{S}$ regulatory cap uses its Rpn11 metalloprotease subunit to slice off and recycle the ubiquitin chain for reuse. Simultaneously, a hexameric ring of six AAA+ ATPase motors (Rpt1–Rpt6) grabs an unstructured peptide tail. Pulling with $11\text{ pN}$ of force, the ring unravels folded domains into the $1.3\text{-nm}$ pore.

· · ·

Inside the central cavity of the two inner $\beta_7$ rings sit six proteolytic active sites whose catalytic nucleophile is the N-terminal threonine (Thr1) of each $\beta1$, $\beta2$, and $\beta5$ subunit. Combining three distinct cleavage specificities, the chamber chops the threaded polypeptide into 7- to 9-amino-acid peptides. Those short fragments match the exact length required for MHC Class I immune display.

· · ·

Synthesized by Julian Adams and colleagues and approved in 2003, the dipeptide boronic acid drug bortezomib (Velcade) locks the catalytic Thr1 hydroxyl of the proteasome's $\beta5$ subunit into a tetrahedral boron adduct. Malignant multiple myeloma plasma cells synthesize massive amounts of misfolded antibody chains. Clogging their proteasomes triggers lethal endoplasmic-reticulum stress while sparing normal tissues.
