Self-Transcendence · Chapter 4

Entropic Brain Flattening

How Relaxing Top-Down Priors Dissolves the Ego's Control Hierarchy

To keep a biological organism alive inside a noisy sensory world without exhausting its energy reserves, the human brain cannot build every perception from the ground up. Instead, the cerebral cortex operates as a hierarchical Bayesian prediction engine. High-level association areas continuously broadcast top-down structural assumptions—known as priors—downward to constrain lower sensory cortices. Only the mismatch between what the top-down model predicted and what the retinas or cochlea actually registered travels back up the hierarchy as a prediction error.

At the very apex of this predictive hierarchy sits the brain's most deeply grooved prior: the model of a stable, bounded self. In 2019, neuroscientist Robin Carhart-Harris at Imperial College London and physicist-neurobiologist Karl Friston at University College London formalized how this hierarchy dissolves in their Relaxed Beliefs Under Psychedelics (REBUS) and entropic brain framework. Under ordinary waking consciousness, deep layer-V pyramidal neurons in the posterior cingulate and medial prefrontal cortices maintain high synaptic gain, or precision weighting, over the ego prior. Synchronous 8-to-13-hertz alpha-band oscillations carry these top-down inhibitory predictions downward. They keep sensory boundaries rigid and filter out competing bottom-up signals.

When serotonergic compounds or deep contemplative states alter the excitability of layer-V pyramidal neurons, those deep projection cells lose their synchronous alpha-band rhythm. In magnetoencephalography (MEG) recordings conducted at Imperial College London between 2013 and 2016, Carhart-Harris, Suresh Muthukumaraswamy, and David Nutt found a striking correlation. Subjective ratings of ego dissolution tracked directly with the collapse of 8-to-13-hertz alpha power inside the posterior cingulate cortex.

When the precision weight assigned to top-down priors drops, the brain's variational free-energy landscape flattens. Deep attractor valleys that normally lock thought into habitual ego channels become shallow, allowing neural trajectories to explore a wider state space. To quantify this flattening, researchers compute the Shannon and Lempel-Ziv entropy $H(X)$ of discretized cortical microstates $x_i$ alongside the precision-weighted prediction error $\varepsilon_l$ at cortical level $l$:

$$H(X) = -\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{N} P(x_i)\log_2 P(x_i), \qquad \varepsilon_l = \Pi_l \bigl(\mu_{l-1} - g(\mu_l)\bigr)$$

Here $P(x_i)$ denotes the empirical probability of observing neural state pattern $x_i$ across $N$ possible microstates. In the second expression, $\mu_{l-1}$ represents the incoming bottom-up signal from level $l-1$, $g(\mu_l)$ is the top-down generative prediction descending from level $l$, and $\Pi_l$ is the synaptic precision weight governing how strongly that level enforces its model. When top-down precision $\Pi_{\text{prior}}$ at the highest cortical levels drops toward zero, high-level ego expectations no longer suppress ascending signals. Consequently, cortical state entropy $H(X)$ rises measurably above normal waking consciousness.

In 2017, Michael Schartner, Anil Seth, and Carhart-Harris applied Abraham Lempel and Jacob Ziv's 1976 mathematical data-compression algorithm to human MEG recordings. General anesthesia and non-rapid-eye-movement sleep reduce Lempel-Ziv signal complexity far below waking baseline. By contrast, ego-dissolving states pushed spontaneous cortical signal diversity significantly above normal waking awareness. The brain did not degenerate into random static. Rather, relaxing the top-down ego hub allowed distant sensory, emotional, and memory networks that rarely communicate in adult life to exchange signals directly. In 2012, Robin Carhart-Harris and David Nutt injected two milligrams of intravenous psilocybin into fifteen healthy volunteers inside an arterial spin labeling MRI scanner at Imperial College London. Expecting to see an explosion of cortical blood flow, they found zero brain regions with increased perfusion and instead recorded a twenty percent drop in blood flow directly inside the thalamus, anterior cingulate, and posterior cingulate ego hubs.

Primary Anchor & Verified Finding
Historical / Experimental Grounding: Robin Carhart-Harris, David Nutt, and colleagues' 2012–2016 Imperial College London arterial spin labeling fMRI and MEG studies, and Carhart-Harris and Karl Friston's 2019 REBUS ('Relaxed Beliefs Under Psychedelics') mathematical formulation.
Empirical Payoff: When Robin Carhart-Harris and David Nutt injected two milligrams of intravenous psilocybin into fifteen healthy volunteers inside an MRI scanner at Imperial College London in 2012 expecting to see an explosion of brain activity, they found zero brain regions with increased blood flow—and instead observed a twenty percent drop in blood flow directly inside the thalamus, anterior cingulate, and posterior cingulate ego hubs.
