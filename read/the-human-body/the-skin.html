The Human Body · Chapter 8

The Skin

Brick-and-Mortar Dead Cells, Evaporative Sweating, and Ultraviolet Vitamin Factories

The outer waterproof shield of your body—the stratum corneum—is just 15 micrometers thick, thinner than household kitchen plastic wrap. It consists of fifteen stacked layers of flattened dead corneocytes packed with keratin filaments and glued together in a hydrophobic lipid mortar of ceramides, cholesterol, and free fatty acids.

· · ·

Humans can outrun horses and antelopes in summer heat because we replaced insulating fur with two to four million eccrine sweat glands. A galloping mammal must breathe once per stride, preventing rapid panting. A human runner decouples breathing from stride and evaporates 3 liters of sweat per hour, shedding 1,700 watts of thermal heat.

· · ·

Fingertips wrinkle in warm bathwater after five minutes not because dead skin swells like a passive sponge, but because sympathetic nerves actively constrict glomus blood vessels beneath the ridges. Surgeons proved in the 1930s that this wrinkling reflex disappears completely if the median nerve to the hand is severed.

· · ·

Neurobiologist Mark Changizi demonstrated in 2011 that autonomic fingertip wrinkling acts just like rain treads on an automobile tire. The branching valleys channel water out from under the finger pads during grip pressure, sluicing away the lubricating film to improve wet-object handling without reducing dry touch sensitivity.

· · ·

Vitamin D3 (cholecalciferol) is not a true dietary vitamin, but a secosteroid hormone synthesized inside the epidermis by sunlight. When a single 290–315 nanometer UVB photon strikes 7-dehydrocholesterol in keratinocytes, it snaps open the molecule's B-ring in a non-enzymatic reaction that the liver and kidneys then activate.

· · ·

Anthropologist Nina Jablonski showed in 1991 that human skin pigmentation across latitude balances two ultraviolet threats. Near the equator, dark eumelanin acts as a natural sunscreen to prevent UVA from destroying blood folate needed for fetal neural tubes. Near the poles, lighter skin admits enough weak UVB to make Vitamin D for pelvic bone growth.

· · ·

When ultraviolet light damages DNA in epidermal keratinocytes, nearby melanocytes manufacture dark melanin granules inside melanosomes and inject them into thirty-six surrounding keratinocytes. Inside each keratinocyte, motor proteins assemble the melanin granules like miniature parasols—supranuclear caps—directly over the sunny side of the cell nucleus.

· · ·

Why tattoo ink stays permanently in place even though the epidermis sheds 40,000 cells every minute: tattoo needles punch 1.5 millimeters deep into the dermis. Dermal macrophages swallow the insoluble pigment particles and, when they die weeks later, release the ink on the spot to be immediately re-swallowed by replacement macrophages.

· · ·

Your skin's surface maintains a protective acid mantle at pH 4.7 to 5.5 from lactic acid in sweat and free fatty acids in sebum. This mild acidity nurtures commensal Staphylococcus epidermidis bacteria while inhibiting pathogenic Staphylococcus aureus and Candida yeasts, which thrive only at neutral pH.

· · ·

Packed into one square centimeter of your index fingertip are 250 Meissner corpuscles (detecting 30-Hertz slip flutter) and 500 Merkel disks (measuring static edges). When a human finger sweeps across an optically smooth glass plate, those mechanoreceptors can detect a single raised bump just 13 nanometers high.

· · ·

Goosebumps are triggered by tiny smooth-muscle bundles (arrector pili) pulling hair follicles upright when sympathetic nerves fire. In furry mammals, this fluffs the coat to trap warm air and make a threatened animal look 30 percent larger; in humans, it survives as a reflex that also stimulates follicle stem cells.

· · ·

Anatomist Karl Langer mapped dermal tension in 1861 by puncturing cadaver skin with a round awl and observing narrow elliptical slits. Because dermal collagen fibers run in directional tension cables rather than a random mesh, surgeons who cut parallel to Langer's lines leave fine hairline scars, while crosswise cuts gape open.
