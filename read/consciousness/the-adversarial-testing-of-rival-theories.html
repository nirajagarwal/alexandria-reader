Consciousness · Chapter 12

The Adversarial Testing of Rival Theories

What Happened When Global Workspace and Integrated Information Theory Faced the Same Data

At a consciousness conference in Bremen, Germany, in June 1998, neuroscientist Christof Koch made a public wager with philosopher David Chalmers. Inspired by rapid advances in single-unit primate electrophysiology and human functional neuroimaging, Koch bet a case of fine wine that within twenty-five years—by June 2023—science would discover a clear, universally accepted neural signature of consciousness in the brain. Chalmers, skeptical that empirical correlation alone would bridge the theoretical divide, took the opposing side.

As the twenty-five-year deadline approached, the field had not converged on a single mechanism. Instead, consciousness neuroscience had split into rival theoretical camps. On one side stood Stanislas Dehaene's Global Neuronal Workspace Theory (GNWT), which locates conscious broadcast in prefrontal-parietal ignition. On the other side stood Giulio Tononi's Integrated Information Theory (IIT), which locates phenomenal experience in a posterior cortical hot zone spanning visual and temporal areas. Worse, because each laboratory designed its own behavioral tasks and statistical pipelines, advocates could always explain away awkward data after the fact.

In 2019, neuroscientists Lucia Melloni, Liad Mudrik, and Michael Pitts organized the Cogitate Consortium—an open-science adversarial collaboration funded by the Templeton World Charity Foundation—to force Dehaene and Tononi into a pre-registered head-to-head test. Before a single volunteer was scanned, both theorists signed off on an identical experimental protocol. Across six independent laboratories, researchers presented supraliminal faces, objects, letters, and false-font symbols for exact durations of 0.5, 1.0, and 1.5 seconds to 256 human participants (120 in fMRI, 102 in magnetoencephalography, and 34 epilepsy patients with intracranial cortical electrodes), locking in three decisive tests:

  • * Stimulus Maintenance Duration (0.5 s vs. 1.0 s vs. 1.5 s): IIT predicted sustained neural firing and decoding across the posterior visual hot zone for the entire duration an image stays consciously visible. GNWT, by contrast, predicted a brief onset ignition burst (~300 ms) followed by silent synaptic maintenance until a second ignition burst at stimulus offset.
  • * Prefrontal Content Decoding (Category vs. Orientation): GNWT predicted that the prefrontal workspace must decode every consciously perceived attribute, including both object category and grating orientation. IIT predicted that orientation can remain conscious inside posterior visual cortex without prefrontal representation.
  • * Inter-Areal Network Synchrony: IIT required sustained gamma- and beta-band phase synchronization linking early visual cortex (V1/V2) with ventral temporal fusiform areas throughout the conscious percept. GNWT instead required long-range fronto-parietal synchrony during workspace ignition.

When the Cogitate Consortium unveiled its results in June 2023 and published the full peer-reviewed dataset in Nature in April 2025, the pre-registered ledger dealt sharp blows to both theories. Posterior visual areas faithfully tracked the full 0.5-, 1.0-, and 1.5-second duration of conscious percepts and decoded stimulus orientation as IIT predicted. However, the intracranial recordings failed to find the sustained posterior inter-areal synchronization that IIT required for causal integration. Conversely, the prefrontal cortex strongly decoded stimulus category at onset as GNWT predicted, but failed to decode conscious stimulus orientation and lacked the predicted second ignition burst when the stimulus turned off. Neither workspace broadcast nor posterior integration survived the adversarial test unscathed. At the twenty-sixth annual meeting of the Association for the Scientific Study of Consciousness at New York University in June 2023, the Cogitate Consortium presented its three-modality results across 256 human subjects. Immediately afterward, Christof Koch walked onto the auditorium stage and handed David Chalmers a bottle of 1978 Portuguese Madeira wine to settle their twenty-five-year wager.

Primary Anchor & Verified Finding
Historical / Experimental Grounding: Christof Koch and David Chalmers's June 1998 Bremen wager, the 2019–2025 Cogitate Consortium adversarial collaboration across 256 human fMRI, MEG, and iEEG subjects led by Lucia Melloni, Liad Mudrik, and Michael Pitts, and the June 2023 ASSC public settlement in New York.
Empirical Payoff: At the twenty-sixth annual meeting of the Association for the Scientific Study of Consciousness at New York University in June 2023, immediately after the Cogitate Consortium presented its three-modality results showing that both Global Neuronal Workspace Theory and Integrated Information Theory failed key pre-registered predictions across 256 human subjects, Christof Koch walked onto the auditorium stage and handed David Chalmers a bottle of 1978 Portuguese Madeira wine to settle their twenty-five-year wager.
