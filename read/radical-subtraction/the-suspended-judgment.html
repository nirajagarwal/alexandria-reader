Radical Subtraction · Chapter 11

The Suspended Judgment

Pyrrho of Elis, Sextus Empiricus, and the Quietness That Follows Equipollence

Around 200 CE, the Greek empiricist physician Sextus Empiricus opened his handbook Outlines of Pyrrhonism with a story from a fourth-century BCE painting studio. The celebrated Hellenistic artist Apelles of Kos was painting a warhorse returning from battle. He had rendered the horse's musculature, hooves, and flared nostrils with lifelike precision, but he could not capture the fleck of bloody foam gathering around the iron bit in the horse's mouth. Every time he applied fine bristle brushes to layer white and red tempera, the result looked stiff and artificial. Driven to exhaustion by the failure of his deliberate technique, Apelles gave up, seized the wet sponge he used to wipe his brushes clean, and flung it against the panel. Where the sponge struck the wood, the mixed pigments left an exact, natural imprint of horse foam.

To Sextus Empiricus, Apelles's sponge illustrated the central discovery of Pyrrho of Elis, who had traveled with Alexander the Great's military expedition to Taxila and northwest India between 327 and 325 BCE. In India, according to the biographer Diogenes Laertius, Pyrrho observed ascetics whom the Greeks called gymnosophists (naked philosophers) and returned to Greece with a radical critique of philosophical dogmatism. Every Greek school in Athens—Platonists, Aristotelians, Epicureans, and Stoics—promised its students that mental peace (ataraxia) would be achieved by discovering the correct dogmatic blueprint of the universe and regulating life according to its truths.

Pyrrho and Sextus discovered the exact inverse. The very effort to establish dogmatic certainty is the primary generator of human anxiety. Whoever holds a dogmatic belief that a specific status, possession, or metaphysical claim is intrinsically good or evil is perpetually tormented. The believer is either terrified of losing what they have, or frantic to acquire what they lack.

To extinguish this cognitive restlessness, Pyrrhonian skepticism operated as a three-step subtractive mechanism:

  • * Isostheneia (Equipollence): For every dogmatic claim about the hidden nature of things—whether derived from sensory appearance or deductive syllogism—the skeptic constructs an opposing argument of equal evidentiary force. Using the Ten Modes of Aenesidemus and the Five Modes of Agrippa (showing that every dogmatic proof falls into infinite regress, circularity, or arbitrary axiom), the investigator balances the cognitive scale until net evidentiary weight equals zero.
  • * Epoche (Suspension of Judgment): Confronted with two mutually cancelling arguments of identical weight ($W_{+} = W_{-}$), the analytical intellect can neither affirm nor deny. It halts its decisive grasp and suspends judgment.
  • * Ataraxia (Untroubled Quietness): The instant the intellect stops straining to adjudicate hidden essences, chronic sympathetic anxiety collapses on its own. Calm follows suspension of judgment automatically, as Sextus wrote, the way a shadow follows a body in sunlight.

Sextus compared this cognitive purge to a self-eliminating laxative drug in humoral medicine. Unlike food, which remains inside the digestive tract and adds mass to the body, a purgative expels toxic humors from the intestines and then flushes itself out along with them. In the same way, the skeptical formula ou mallon—stating that nothing is more this way than that way—cancels dogmatic assertions and then cancels itself, leaving no new creed behind.

Crucially, Pyrrhonian subtraction did not paralyze everyday action. As a practicing medical doctor, Sextus Empiricus treated patients daily by following immediate sensory appearances, bodily hunger and thirst, local civic laws, and empirical medical craft. He administered remedies that had worked in clinical observation without claiming to know the hidden metaphysical essence of disease. By subtracting only the extra layer of dogmatic opinion, the skeptic navigated the physical world with full motor competence and zero existential friction.
