Contemplative Theory

Radical Subtraction

Sensory Isolation, Negative Theology, and the Quiet Mind

Most intellectual traditions treat human understanding as a problem of accumulation. To solve an impasse or comprehend reality, the student is instructed to gather more empirical observations, master a larger technical vocabulary, and stack affirmative propositions into an ever-taller conceptual edifice. Across science, law, and theology, progress is ordinarily equated with addition.

Yet a recurrent discovery runs across distant centuries and independent philosophical lineages, from the stone anchorholds of medieval Europe and the monastic debate courtyards of ancient India to the skeptical academies of the Hellenistic Mediterranean and the isolation laboratories of twentieth-century psychology. Whenever investigators sought to observe the baseline architecture of consciousness or touch an unconditioned ground of truth, they discovered that adding concepts only thickened the obstruction.

Human waking awareness is chronically crowded by sensory stimulation, social ambition, autobiographical memory, and the grammatical habit of dividing every experience into a grasping subject and a bounded object. Each new philosophical definition or imaginative picture introduces another filter between the observer and reality.

To clear the channel, these thinkers inverted the ordinary direction of inquiry. Rather than constructing a richer representation inside the mind, they practiced systematic subtraction—removing sensory inputs, dismantling false identifications, balancing opposing arguments until dogmatic judgment halted, and chiseling away conceptual predicates. Whether executed through physical enclosure, four-cornered logical negation, non-instrumental sitting, or the suspension of belief, subtraction operates on a single principle: what is fundamental does not need to be manufactured from scratch, because it becomes visible the moment superfluous noise is taken away.

  • 1. The Cell on the Tagus
    John of the Cross and the Two Dark Nights in Toledo
    Juan de Yepes y Álvarez locked inside a six-by-ten-foot monastic closet adjacent to a privy in Toledo from December 1577 to August 1578, composing the Spiritual Canticle in total darkness before formalizing his treatises Subida del Monte Carmelo and Noche Oscura.
  • 2. The Walled-In Window
    Julian of Norwich and the Architecture of the Medieval Anchorhold
    Julian of Norwich sealed inside a twelve-foot stone cell attached to St. Julian's Church in Norwich through the liturgical Rite of Enclosure following her May 1373 illness, writing the Short Text and Long Text of Revelations of Divine Love during the recurring waves of the Black Death and the 1381 Peasants' Revolt.
  • 3. The Ground Beyond Names
    Meister Eckhart, Gelassenheit, and the Cloud of Unknowing
    Dominican master Johannes Eckhart preaching in Middle High German in Erfurt, Strasbourg, and Cologne before his 1326–1328 inquisitorial trials in Cologne and Avignon, paired with the anonymous late-fourteenth-century English author of The Cloud of Unknowing.
  • 4. The Four-Cornered Negation
    Nagarjuna and the Dismantling of Independent Essence at Nalanda
    Second-century CE South Indian Buddhist philosopher Nagarjuna composing the Mulamadhyamakakarika (Fundamental Verses on the Middle Way), the foundational logical treatise studied for centuries across the monastic university of Nalanda.
  • 5. Not This, Not This
    Adi Shankara, the Upanishads, and the Peeling Away of the False Self
    Eighth-century CE Indian philosopher Adi Shankara writing his commentaries (bhashyas) on the Brihadaranyaka Upanishad and the Brahma Sutras, formalizing the Advaita Vedanta method of neti neti.
  • 6. Just Sitting at Eihei-ji
    Eihei Dogen and the Subtraction of Goal-Directed Meditation
    Japanese monk Eihei Dogen training under Caodong master Tiantong Rujing in Song Dynasty China in 1225–1227 before returning to Japan to write the Fukanzazengi and Shobogenzo and founding Eihei-ji in the snowy mountains of Echizen in 1244.
  • 7. The Citadel of Choice
    Epictetus and the Stoic Subtraction of External Fortune
    Epictetus born into slavery in Hierapolis around 50 CE, serving in Nero's Rome under the imperial freedman Epaphroditus before founding his philosophical school at Nicopolis in Epirus after Emperor Domitian expelled philosophers from Italy around 89 CE.
  • 8. Decreation and Waiting
    Simone Weil on Unmuscular Attention and the Emptying of the Ego
    French philosopher Simone Weil taking a leave from lycée teaching in 1934–1935 to work as a power-press operator at the Alsthom and Renault factories near Paris, and later filling her lined composition notebooks in Marseilles between 1941 and May 1942.
  • 9. The Uncarved Block
    Zhuangzi, Butcher Ding's Ox Cleaver, and the Removal of Friction
    Third-century BCE Warring States philosophical text Zhuangzi (Chapter 3, The Mastery of Nurturing Life), recording the dialogue between Butcher Ding and Lord Wenhui of Liang.
  • 10. The Sculptor's Chisel
    Plotinus, Pseudo-Dionysius, and the Removal of Conceptual Marble
    Plotinus teaching in Rome around 250 CE and writing Enneads I.6.9, paired with the early-sixth-century Syrian monk who composed the Mystical Theology in Greek under the pseudonym Dionysius the Areopagite.
  • 11. The Suspended Scale
    Pyrrho of Elis, Sextus Empiricus, and the Calm That Follows Equipollence
    Pyrrho of Elis traveling with Alexander the Great's campaign to northwest India between 327 and 325 BCE, and Greek empiricist physician Sextus Empiricus codifying Outlines of Pyrrhonism around 200 CE with the parable of the Hellenistic painter Apelles.
  • 12. The Kicked-Away Ladder and the Soundproof Tank
    From Wittgenstein's Tractatus to the Sensory Deprivation Experiments of McGill
    Ludwig Wittgenstein writing the Tractatus Logico-Philosophicus in military notebooks on the Eastern and Italian fronts between 1914 and 1918, juxtaposed with Donald Hebb, Woodburn Heron, and B. K. Doane's 1951–1954 sensory deprivation cubicles at McGill University in Montreal.
